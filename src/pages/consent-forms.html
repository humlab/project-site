Consent forms for people with limited previous education

2026

Informed consent is a fundamental part of ethical research. However, written consent forms can be a barrier for refugees with limited literacy or proficiency in the local language, potentially making it difficult for them to fully understand their rights and what taking part in research involves.

To address this challenge, the project developed presentation materials and a series of short explanatory videos designed to make information about research participation and informed consent clearer and more accessible, particularly for refugees with limited literacy. The work was carried out at Umeå University as a user project within InfraVis. Read more about the project at InfraVis

The three videos explain participants' rights and the key conditions of research participation through visual storytelling. They were created using Adobe Illustrator, After Effects and Premiere Pro, and subtitled and dubbed into six languages commonly spoken among SFI students: Swedish, Arabic, Swahili, Dari, Persian (Farsi) and Tigrinya.

The videos and accompanying presentation materials were designed to complement, not replace, personal interaction with researchers and interpreters. By making research information more accessible, the project supported participants in understanding their rights, asking relevant questions and making informed decisions about whether to take part in a study. The illustrations on this page are by Linnéa Tjernström, who produced the videos.
