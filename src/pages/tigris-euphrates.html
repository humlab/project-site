GIS visualization of archaeological sites in the upper Tigris and Euphrates River basins

2025

The shift from hunting and gathering to farming left its trace in the landscape as settlement sites, tools, buildings and the remains of the animals people ate. This project turned that record into printed maps: a GIS-based visualization of archaeological sites in the upper reaches of the Tigris and Euphrates, which brings archaeological, environmental and material culture data into one spatial picture of how people and their environment changed together. Sites in the South Levant and on Cyprus were visualized the same way, and diagrams place the material culture from three sites in the upper Tigris basin in time.

The researchers supplied the data as Excel files: site coordinates, environmental conditions, and what had been found at each place, from tools and architectural remains to the animals people had eaten. The files were cleaned and organised for ArcGIS Pro, where the spatial data was visualized with the 3D Analyst extension and where elevation and environmental layers were supplemented with data from SRTM, the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission. Text, symbols and final design were added in Adobe Illustrator and the other Adobe tools. Dimitrios Staikoglou carried out the work as the InfraVis application expert assigned to the project, with Maria Podkorytova as InfraVis node coordinator at Umeå University.

The result is a series of printed maps and diagrams for Yasemin Menemenli's research into the shift to farming, led by Ing-Marie Back Danielsson. Read more about the project at InfraVis. The maps are by Yasemin Menemenli and Dimitrios Staikoglou, © 2025.
